Psycholinguistic Feature-Based Depression Analysis

Objective

The objective of this component was to investigate whether psychological and linguistic characteristics of social-media text could provide useful signals for distinguishing between depression severity classes.

Unlike contextual embeddings, this approach focuses on interpretable characteristics of language rather than representing each text only through learned semantic features.

Work Completed

The psycholinguistic component was developed as a separate modelling approach within the larger project.

Its purpose is to investigate patterns in how people express themselves, including linguistic characteristics that may be associated with different depression labels.

The aim is to complement conventional text representations with features that can be inspected and interpreted.

The exact feature extraction methods, classifier, and evaluation results should be filled in from the actual implementation rather than assumed.

Limitations

* Linguistic patterns are not definitive indicators of a person’s mental health.
* Writing style varies across individuals, platforms, and contexts.
* Some features may overlap across the three classes.
* Class imbalance may affect the model’s ability to identify minority classes.
* Feature usefulness must be established empirically rather than assumed from psychological interpretations.

Expected Contribution

This component explores an interpretable, feature-driven perspective on depression classification. Its results can later be compared with the RoBERTa component to investigate whether the two approaches capture different kinds of information.
